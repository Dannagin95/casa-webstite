OUR PROJECT'S JOURNAL

A journey through our projects, where wood flooring quietly completes each spatial experience.

The Quiet Foundation: When Wood Flooring Becomes a Shared Language Across Distinct Spaces

A perspective drawn from built projects by CASA Parquet, where material helps shape the experience and identity of each space.

We often remember a building through what is most immediately visible: a shaft of light falling through the interior, a curve in the architecture, or the sense of refinement on entering a main lobby. Less attention is given to the surface beneath each step — a quiet foundation that connects those experiences into a coherent whole.

Across projects ranging from showrooms and penthouses to hotels and museums, CASA Parquet has found that every space carries a distinct identity. There is no single formula for every project, and no material that suits every context. The difference lies in choosing materials to serve the architecture rather than compete to become its focal point.

Where Does a Space Begin?

Some begin with the first drawing. Others begin with light, proportion, or the way walls define volume. Each approach has its own logic. Yet as a project moves toward completion, the question shifts from "What will this space look like?"to "How will this space feel?".

That is when material begins to speak — not through display, but through the feeling underfoot, the way light reaches the wood surface, the continuity between functional zones and the ability to remain with the building through years of use. A well-resolved floor does not change the architecture, but it can change how that architecture is experienced every day.

“Some spaces are made for people to pause, observe and feel. There, material does not seek the centre of attention; it quietly completes the experiences worth remembering.”

“If some places are remembered after a single visit, others are defined by what happens within them every day.”

For CASA Parquet, every project is a reminder that material never exists in isolation. Its value is fully realised only when it becomes a natural part of the space — appropriate to its function, its rhythm of use and the story the architecture seeks to tell.

Each Project, a Different Perspective

Although the projects belong to different architectural typologies, they all raise the same question: what role should the material play in this space? At times, the floor needs to become a neutral foundation that allows light to lead the experience. Elsewhere, it connects functional areas within a home. In other projects, its value is measured by the ability to support thousands of footsteps every day.

Each project below represents a different point in the journey of CASA Parquet. The aim is not to find one universal answer, but to show how a flooring system can adapt to different contexts, functions and architectural narratives.

When Light Leads the Space

Entering ADES Lighting Showroom, the first impression does not come from the material underfoot, but from the way light is controlled throughout the interior. Display lighting, reflective surfaces and architectural curves are composed to guide the eye and bring focus to the products.

In this context, the wood floor does not need to become another focal point. A balanced foundation instead connects light, furnishings and architecture into a continuous whole. When the material recedes at the right moment, the space feels more coherent and the user experience becomes more natural.

This is also part of how CASA Parquet approaches each project. The value of wood flooring lies not only in the beauty of individual boards, but in its ability to integrate with the architecture and support what the project is trying to express. Sometimes a material is remembered not because it stands out, but because it makes everything around it feel more complete.

ADES LIGHTING - SHOWROOM

A Space to Live In, Not Only to Look At

Unlike a showroom, where every detail is composed around a visual experience, a penthouse is shaped by everyday life. Material choices must balance appearance with long-term use, because the beauty of a home is not defined only at completion, but by how it continues to support daily living over time.

At Penthouse, the wood flooring system was selected to become a continuous part of the living space. The Herringbone layout establishes a rhythm between functional zones, while the natural wood surface brings warmth and balance alongside large areas of glazing, natural stone and contemporary furnishings. Rather than dividing the interior, the floor helps connect its different areas into one coherent whole.

For CASA Parquet, the quality of a living space is not judged only through finished photographs, but through how it feels after years of use. Each flooring solution is therefore considered in relation to the way people live, move and form a relationship with the home, allowing the material to retain value through everyday life rather than only at handover.

PENTHOUSE

Value Revealed Through Everyday Movement

A hotel may take only minutes to make an impression, but it takes years to demonstrate its quality. After opening day, the building enters a different phase of life in which every surface must adapt to continuous use and demanding daily operations.

For the flooring system, the requirement extends beyond initial appearance. The construction must remain stable as environmental conditions change, while the surface finish should preserve the natural feel of wood and support practical maintenance throughout operation. These qualities are rarely visible at first glance, yet they become decisive after years of use.

High-intensity environments such as hotels make one point especially clear: materials do not stop serving the architecture at handover. They continue to perform every day. When appearance, stability and maintainability endure together, a flooring system begins to demonstrate its real value.

KHÁCH SẠN 5 SAO

Some Spaces Are Made for Quiet Attention

Not every building is intended to make an immediate visual statement. Some are designed for people to move more slowly, look more carefully and give greater attention to what is being presented. In a museum, architecture does not tell the story in place of the exhibits; it creates a sufficiently quiet context in which that story can be fully received.

Material therefore needs to be equally measured in its presence. Wood grain extending across the floor is not there to attract attention, but to connect galleries, guide movement and keep the experience continuous from beginning to end. Every detail works toward the same objective: supporting the value of the space rather than competing to become its focal point.

Perhaps this is when a flooring system fulfils its role most completely: not when people stop to look at it, but when its presence almost disappears because it has already become part of the architecture, the light and the stories held within the building.

BẢO TÀNG CỔ VẬT

One Material, Many Ways of Belonging

From a showroom where light is central to the experience, to a penthouse shaped by everyday life, a hotel in constant operation or a museum preserving cultural value, each project places very different demands on material. There is no single answer for every space, and no flooring system can suit every context without an understanding of the architecture and the way people will use it.

This is also how CASA Parquet understands the role of material. A flooring system is not created simply to complete a surface, but to become part of the lived experience, the operation of the building and the stories each space carries forward over time.
